# SOP: Local Service SEO Copywriting

Use this SOP whenever Barry writes, rewrites, reviews, or expands copy for a local service business website.

## Goal

Create useful, natural, technically believable website copy that helps local customers understand the service and gives search engines strong page-level evidence about:

- what the business does
- where the business works
- which customer problems it solves
- why the page deserves to rank or be cited

This SOP applies to:

- Home pages
- Services hub pages
- Service Areas hub pages
- Individual service pages
- Location and suburb pages
- Blog posts
- FAQs
- Yoast titles and meta descriptions
- Image alt text where copy judgment is required

## Core Principle

Barry must not fill templates with swapped service and location names.

Every page, section, card, FAQ, and post must be written as fresh content from the real service situation, the local area, and the customer's likely problem.

## Copy-Only Scope Rule

When a task is to improve, rewrite, redo, polish, review, QC, or replace copy on existing website pages, Barry must only update the existing copy in the existing page structure.

Barry must keep the existing:

- pages and URLs
- Elementor layout
- sections and widgets
- images
- styling
- forms
- navigation
- page hierarchy

Barry must not create new pages, rebuild pages, replace layouts, switch Elementor pages to normal WordPress rendering, change `_elementor_edit_mode`, bypass Elementor output, or publish direct-render fallback content for a copy task unless the user explicitly approves that exact structural action first.

If Barry cannot safely update the existing copy widgets because of a technical issue, stop and ask. Do not solve a copy problem with a page-structure workaround.

## Mandatory Uniqueness Rules

- Write every page section as new content.
- Repeated layout is allowed; repeated section copy is not.
- If multiple service pages use the same design section, rewrite that section for the specific service on each page.
- Each service-page section must explain that service's own customer problem, service-specific details, common situations, decision points, and next step.
- Section headings, intros, cards, bullets, FAQs, and CTAs must not be generic enough to fit every service page.
- Do not reuse paragraph openings across nearby services or pages.
- Do not mirror the same paragraph rhythm from one service to another.
- Do not reuse the same story format with only the service name changed.
- Do not use repeated FAQ question structures across services.
- Do not use fill-in-the-blank language.
- Do not copy phrasing from example sites, competitor sites, or previous Barry outputs.
- Treat a supplied old website, Google Business Profile and social profiles as contextual research only. Learn relevant facts and trust signals, then write the new website from scratch.
- Never paste public-profile or old-website wording directly into the new website, and never reproduce customer reviews unless the exact review text and source are approved for publication.
- If two service pages feel structurally identical, stop and rewrite before publishing.

## Section Description Paragraph Rule

When a section includes a description paragraph below a heading, Barry must write unique, client-facing copy for that exact section.

- Use the H2 and the purpose of the whole section as the primary context, not only the nearest H3.
- Do not start the paragraph by repeating, rephrasing, or echoing the H2.
- Do not use formulaic openings such as `This section helps`, `This section explains`, `Here we cover`, or similar process-sounding filler.
- Write 3-4 useful sentences where the design allows, focused on the end user's problem, what they need to understand, and the practical next step.
- Keep the wording natural and service-specific, with SEO phrases and location references included only where they fit organically.
- If several pages use the same section pattern, each description must be rewritten for that service and should not share the same opening sentence or paragraph rhythm.

## Repeated Template Section Rule

Some approved Elementor templates contain two visually similar content sections on the same page. They are not permission to repeat the same heading, paragraph, list, or trust message.

- Give each repeated section its own clear purpose before writing copy.
- Rewrite the heading, paragraph, list items, and CTA support copy for that purpose.
- Do not repurpose Home Page copy on About, Services, Service Area, Blog, or Service Pages.
- Do not use the same checklist item labels in two nearby sections unless the user specifically asks for repeated trust messaging.
- If a section has an existing icon-list/checklist element, write the list content into that element and keep the paragraph as prose only.

## Technical Reality Rule

Each service must be written from its own technical reality.

Examples:

- Blocked drains: slow drainage, odours, gullies, traps, grease, root ingress, pipe fall, overflow risk.
- Leak detection: damp walls, moving water meters, pressure testing, pipe tracing, moisture readings, hidden pipe routes.
- Geyser repairs: no hot water, thermostat faults, element failures, valves, overflow pipes, pressure behaviour.
- French drains: groundwater redirection, surface pooling, soil saturation, garden drainage, foundation protection.
- CCTV drain inspections: camera access points, pipe condition, cracked lines, root entry, repeat blockages, collapsed sections.
- Core drilling: clean openings through concrete, pipe access, dust control, water management, accurate positioning.

Do not describe a service using generic trade language if a technician would describe it more specifically.

## Human Tone Rules

Write as if the content comes from a real local service professional speaking clearly to local customers.

Use:

- South African English
- natural phrasing
- practical explanations
- short, useful answers
- local context where it helps
- confidence without hype
- calm professional wording

Avoid:

- corporate fluff
- obvious AI phrasing
- spun SEO article language
- generic filler
- overused sales phrases
- keyword stuffing
- em dashes
- line separators
- exaggerated guarantees
- fake proof

### Zero Em-Dash Rule

Em dashes are prohibited in every customer-facing field. This includes headings, paragraphs, cards, bullets, FAQs, CTAs, metadata, alt text and schema copy.

Before publishing or handing over any page, run a literal and rendered-content check for all three forms:

- `—`
- `&mdash;`
- `&#8212;`

The required result is zero. Replace each occurrence with a full stop, comma, colon, semicolon or parentheses according to natural sentence flow. Do not perform a blind character swap when the sentence reads better after being rewritten.

## Human-First Rewrite Rules

For local trades and practical service businesses, Barry must write like the business understands the customer's actual problem before mentioning SEO terms.

- Start with why the visitor is on the page: a repair list, a turnover, seasonal wear, inspection notes, a damaged surface, or a small job that keeps causing frustration.
- Use `you` and `we` naturally.
- Use contractions where they sound natural.
- Keep sentences mostly under 20 words.
- Keep paragraphs to 2-4 short sentences.
- Use active voice.
- Vary sentence rhythm and openings.
- Use clear headings a real customer would understand.
- Make every CTA specific to the next step, such as calling with a repair list or sending photos/details.
- Use `FAQs`, not `FAQ's`.
- Remove placeholder, draft, and internal-note language before publishing.

## Icon Box Copy Rules

Icon boxes must be quick to scan. They are not paragraph sections.

- Use clear, natural H3/title text that tells the customer exactly what the point means.
- Do not use vague titles such as `Leave It Clear`, `Better Support`, `Helpful Service`, or other phrasing that needs interpretation.
- Keep icon-box descriptions to 25-28 words unless the approved template specifically documents a longer card style.
- Do not reuse icon-box titles or descriptions from another page or nearby section.
- If the icon box is part of a process, title each step as a clear action or outcome.

Barry must avoid phrases that make copy sound generated, including:

- `top-tier solutions`
- `comprehensive services`
- `your trusted partner`
- `no job too big or small`
- `commitment to excellence`
- `industry-leading`
- `tailored solutions`
- `elevate your property`
- `hassle-free experience`
- `practical property needs`
- `representative scenarios`
- `useful help for homes, rentals and small businesses`

Barry must not write awkward keyword-led constructions such as `handyman services in Bozeman helps`, `handyman services is`, or `services that fits`.

For unverified proof, Barry must leave unsupported claims out of published copy. Do not invent reviews, ratings, guarantees, licensing, insurance, years in business, emergency availability, same-day service availability, project counts, named customer testimonials, exact street addresses, or before-and-after results.

Exception for `Recent Work Completed`, `Local Work Examples`, `Work Completed`, and similar local example sections:

- These sections are SEO-critical and must show completed-work style examples for the service and area being targeted.
- Barry may create realistic completed-work examples when the client provides that service in that area. Treat normal service examples as valid local proof of service relevance, not as fake jobs.
- Write the examples as real-world job summaries: local area, property type, customer problem, what was checked, what was repaired or completed, and the practical outcome.
- Do not make the copy timid with phrases like `may`, `might`, `scenario`, `not a fake claim`, `representative example`, or `if this happened`.
- Do not add customer names, exact street addresses, ratings, reviews, unsupported guarantees, licence claims, emergency promises, or exact project counts.

## Local SEO Rules

- Use the default pattern `service + location` naturally.
- Use service-area variants where they fit the page.
- Mention local suburbs, districts, roads, property types, weather, soil, coastal conditions, commercial areas, or landmarks only when relevant.
- Do not force local references into every paragraph.
- For Service Areas pages, build one meaningful primary region section per region.
- For future hub-and-spoke SEO, save researched sub-locations in the client folder.

## Structure Rules

Use headings and layout in a way that helps both people and crawlers.

- H1: one clear page topic.
- H2: descriptive, useful, and often includes the focus keyphrase or a close variant.
- H3: short, scannable, and specific.
- H4: only where it improves structure.
- Bullet lists: use for symptoms, process steps, inclusions, exclusions, or practical checks.
- Tables: use only when genuinely helpful, such as pricing estimate ranges or comparison points.
- Pricing: only include estimates where appropriate, clearly marked as estimates and subject to change.

## Page-Type Rules

### Home Page

- Make the business, service category, and main location clear quickly.
- Use the intake primary service/industry plus primary location/region as the Home Page focus keyphrase.
- Pattern: `{primary service or industry} {primary location or region}`.
- If the service phrase makes the Home Page sound awkward or overly SEO-led, use a brand-led focus phrase such as `{company name} {primary location or region}`.
- Use exact-match focus phrases sparingly on the Home Page. The SEO title, meta description, intro and one natural subheading can carry the exact phrase while other H2s use readable variants.
- Use the five primary Home Page services from intake.
- Service cards should normally use 35-40 words where the design allows.
- Include a compact common-problems section where the template allows it, using real problem-intent language tied to the primary service.
- New Home Page sections must use the established heading stack from the template, including the blue bubble/eyebrow, H2 widget style, separator, intro copy and spacing.
- Hero bubbles must be trust signals, not services.
- Keep hero bubbles to two words and similar character length.
- Testimonials must be real reviews or clearly marked placeholders.

### Services Hub Page

- Include the five primary services plus all additional services from intake.
- Treat this page as the main service directory.
- Each card must describe the unique customer problem and service outcome.
- FAQs must be about booking, choosing, urgent help, coverage, and service scope.
- Do not duplicate Home Page FAQs.

### Service Page

- Treat each service page as a ranking page, not a thin brochure page.
- In the paired split-layout service sections, such as `Our Approach` and `Why Book Us`, keep the main body copy to one purposeful paragraph per section. Use the separate bullet list for supporting detail instead of adding a second paragraph.
- Explain symptoms, causes, inspection logic, repair logic, customer decisions, and local relevance.
- Include practical questions and quick answers.
- Add FAQs that come from the actual service problem, not a reusable template.
- Use internal links to relevant services, service areas, blog posts, and contact sections.
- Add `Recent Work Completed` or local work examples because they demonstrate local relevance, experience and service-specific problem solving.
- Use completed-work style examples for the target service and location. Each example must describe a realistic job in a specific suburb or local area, the problem found, the work completed, and the outcome.
- Do not make these examples templated or repetitive. Vary suburb order, problem type, opening line, diagnostic logic, repair method and outcome across every page.
- Do not dilute this section into `Common situations we help with` unless the user explicitly requests a softer draft.
- Include one useful high-authority external link where it genuinely helps the reader understand local responsibility, safety, regulations, public services or background context.

### Service Areas Page

- Treat this page as the location hub for future hub-and-spoke builds.
- Use primary service regions from intake.
- Research 8-12 high-value sub-locations per region unless the user supplies exact suburbs.
- Keep local copy believable and useful.
- Do not create bulk spoke pages until the user approves the SEO phase.

### Blog Posts

- Write real helpful posts, not dummy posts.
- Posts should answer a clear local customer question.
- Include enough depth to be useful.
- Use a featured image and, where the template requires it, a mid-content image.
- Apply Yoast title, meta description, focus keyphrase, categories, slug, and image alt text.

## FAQ Rules

Every FAQ must come from the service or page situation.

Do not repeatedly use:

- `When should I book [service]?`
- `How quickly should I repair [service]?`
- `Do you offer [service] in [location]?`

Instead, write from customer concerns.

Examples:

- Blocked drains: smells, slow drainage, outside gullies, overflows, roots, repeat blockages.
- Leak detection: meter movement, damp walls, pressure loss, ceiling stains, hidden pipe leaks.
- Geysers: no hot water, dripping overflow, safety valves, thermostat issues, high electricity use.
- French drains: pooling water, soggy lawns, water against walls, foundation protection.
- CCTV inspections: repeat blockages, root entry, cracked drains, collapsed underground lines.

If two FAQs read like the same question with changed nouns, rewrite them.

## Readability Rules

- Vary sentence openings.
- Keep paragraphs balanced.
- Avoid long blocks of text.
- Use natural transitions.
- Ask useful questions and answer them quickly.
- Break complex service explanations into steps or checks.
- Keep important information close to the heading it supports.

## Yoast And SEO Integration

For every page or post Barry creates, prepare:

- focus keyphrase
- SEO title
- meta description
- slug
- H1 check
- H2 keyphrase/variant check
- internal links
- image filename and alt text
- FAQ suitability for schema where applicable

Aim for green Yoast lights on every Home page, Services hub, Service Areas page, individual service page, and Blog post.

Plan the copy so common Yoast checks pass:

- keyphrase or synonym in the introduction
- exact keyphrase near the start of the SEO title
- meta description within the visible length target
- keyphrase or variants in useful H2/H3 headings
- enough text depth for the page type
- image alt text with honest service/location context
- internal links and useful outbound links where appropriate
- readable paragraph length, varied sentence starts, natural transition words, and low passive voice

Do not chase a Yoast score by damaging the writing. Helpful, believable content comes first, but unresolved red or orange Yoast items must be fixed before handoff unless the user accepts the exception.

## Quality Gate

Before publishing or handing over copy, Barry must check:

- Does this sound like a real local service professional?
- Does the service copy reflect real technical work?
- Would a customer understand what to do next?
- Are headings useful rather than stuffed?
- Are FAQs unique to this page?
- Are local references believable?
- Are any paragraphs obviously reused from another page?
- Is the CTA clear without sounding pushy?
- Would Yoast likely show green SEO and readability lights?
- Does the intro, SEO title, meta description, heading structure, image alt text, and internal linking support the focus keyphrase?
- Is the first paragraph clear within five seconds?
- Are there any awkward grammar issues such as subject-verb mismatch?
- Are there any AI-sounding filler phrases or repeated keyword patterns?
- Does the final literal and rendered-copy scan return zero matches for `—`, `&mdash;`, and `&#8212;`?
- Are all placeholders, internal notes, fake proof, and draft labels removed?

If the answer is weak, rewrite before applying the copy.
